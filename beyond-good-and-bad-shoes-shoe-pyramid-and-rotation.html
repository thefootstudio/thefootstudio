Beyond Good and Bad Shoes: The Shoe Pyramid and Shoe Rotation

For a long time, footwear advice was simple: these shoes are good, those shoes are bad. We don’t look at shoes that way anymore. It’s about having the right shoe for the right occasion, and a shoe wardrobe built around the way you live. This is new age podiatry.

Why we moved on from “good” and “bad”

A shoe that’s perfect for a long walk isn’t the shoe you’d wear to a wedding, the beach or the gym. Everyone’s feet, bodies, activities and sensory needs are different, and so are the shoes that suit them.

Not everyone needs bulky lace-up shoes. Research guides our advice, and you know your body and your life best. When shoes fit well and feel right, you’re more likely to actually wear them, and comfort is part of the picture too.

The shoe pyramid

Just like the food pyramid, we start with the right shoes at the base and work our way up. It’s okay to have other shoes “sometimes”.

Base: every day

Your foundation. Well fitted, supportive shoes for what you do most: walking, work and the school run. Measured to fit and comfortable, so you’ll actually wear them.

Middle: for the things you love

Shoes made for the job. Running, hiking, the gym, sport, gardening, the beach and work boots. The right shoe for each activity.

Top: sometimes

Heels, thongs and flat fashion shoes. Enjoy them. When you do, there are often better alternatives, like a thong with a contoured footbed, or a lower, broader heel.

What is shoe rotation?

Shoe rotation simply means having more than one pair in regular use and swapping between them, rather than wearing the same pair every day.

A lesson from athletes

Runners have known this for a while. Many keep a few pairs on the go: one for long runs, one for speed, one for the trails. We love taking ideas like this from sport and bringing them into everyday life.

Pinballing the load

Every shoe loads your feet and legs in a slightly different way. Moving between a few pairs gives your body small, healthy variations in loading, rather than the exact same pattern day after day. Think of it as pinballing the load around.

There’s some research behind this too. In one study of 264 recreational runners followed over 22 weeks, those who used more than one pair of running shoes had a lower rate of running-related injury than those who used one pair (Malisoux et al., Scandinavian Journal of Medicine and Science in Sports, 2015). It was an observational study, so it can’t prove the shoes were the reason, but it supports the idea that variety in how we load our bodies is a good thing.

The right club for the shot

Let’s be honest, it’s really about matching the right shoe to what you’re doing. A golfer doesn’t play the whole course with one club. They carry a kit, and pick the right club for each shot. Your shoes work the same way. A small selection, each matched to an activity, can help you win at life.

Spreading the wear

When two or three pairs share the load, each one wears more slowly, and you’re less likely to keep wearing a pair long after it’s worn out.

Building your shoe rotation

  • Start at the base. Two pairs of everyday shoes you can alternate between is a great place to begin.
  • Add the things you love. Think about what you do each week: walking the dog, a run, the gym, gardening, a long shift. Each may suit a different shoe.
  • Enjoy your sometimes shoes. They have a place too. Just not every day.
  • Get measured. Feet change over the years. Bring the shoes you wear most, and any orthotics or braces, so we can check the fit with them in.

Better for your feet and the planet

Investing in pieces that last is better for your feet and for the planet. When your shoes have had their day, drop them into our shoe recycling bin at the studio.

When to see a podiatrist

If you have foot pain, sensitive feet, diabetes, or you wear orthotics or braces, a podiatry appointment can help you work out what suits you. For everyday fitting advice, our Footwear Fitting Crew measures and fits every pair, following a podiatry fitting process. No appointment is needed for the footwear shop.
