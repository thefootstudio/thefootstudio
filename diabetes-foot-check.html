How diabetes can affect your feet

Your feet rely on a good blood supply to stay healthy, and on lots of nerves that act as a warning system. If you have a stone in your shoe, those nerves tell your brain something is wrong.

Over time, diabetes can affect both. Nerve changes can mean you no longer feel that stone, so a blister, cut or ingrown nail can go unnoticed. Reduced circulation means cuts, burns and blisters take longer to heal. Together, these can let small problems turn into wounds or ulcers, which is why regular checks and daily care matter so much.

Your diabetes neurovascular assessment

This is a thorough check of how diabetes may be affecting your feet, so we can spot changes early and plan your care.

  1. 1

    Your health history

    We talk about your diabetes, general health, medications and any concerns about your feet.

  2. 2

    Nerve checks

    We test sensation, vibration and reflexes to look for any signs of nerve change (neuropathy).

  3. 3

    Circulation checks

    We check your foot pulses and may use Doppler ultrasound and toe pressures to see how well blood is reaching your feet.

  4. 4

    Skin and nails

    We look for dryness, cracks, callus, corns, infection or wounds, and check your nails.

  5. 5

    Movement and walking

    We check joint range, muscle strength and your footwear, and may use gait analysis on our treadmill to look at pressure as you walk.

  6. 6

    Your risk level and action plan

    We explain your foot risk, give you a written action plan and send a report to your GP.

Where needed, we also look after your nails and any callus or corns in the same appointment.

Your foot risk level

Your results help us work out your foot risk, which guides how often we recommend seeing you.

  • Low risk: usually every 6 to 12 months.
  • Medium risk: usually every 3 to 6 months.
  • High risk: usually every 1 to 3 months.

Many people also find regular podiatry nail and skin care helpful between checks, along with a visual inspection of their feet by a professional.

Your personalised action plan

  • How often to come back, based on your risk level
  • Care for anything we find, such as a skin and nail plan for fungal nails or skin infections
  • Further assessments where helpful, such as a biomechanical assessment, orthotic review or footwear fitting in our shoe and movement shop
  • Products that may help, such as pressure-relieving insoles, custom orthoses or compression stockings
  • Whether you can safely look after your own nails at home, or whether you’d like us to help

Looking after your feet at home

  • Check your feet every day, including between your toes. We can show you how.
  • Wash with a gentle, low pH wash and dry well between your toes.
  • Use a urea-based moisturiser on dry skin, but not between your toes.
  • Avoid walking barefoot, even at home.
  • Shake out your shoes before you put them on and check inside for rough edges.
  • Choose lace-up shoes that are deep and wide enough at the toes, with cotton socks.
  • Keep your blood sugar levels in the range your GP or diabetes educator recommends, stay active and avoid smoking.
  • Cover any cut or graze with an antiseptic and a clean dressing each day. If it isn’t healing, see your podiatrist or GP.
Questions

Frequently asked questions

How often should I have my feet checked?

At least once a year, and more often if you have a higher foot risk. We'll recommend what suits you.

Can I use a Medicare referral?

Yes. If your GP has given you a Chronic Condition Management referral, you can use it for podiatry, with a Medicare rebate.

Can I cut my own toenails?

Many people can. If you have reduced feeling, poor circulation or find it hard to reach your feet, we may recommend we look after your nails for you.

What happens if you find a problem?

We'll explain what we've found, start any care you need and work with your GP and other health professionals where needed.

Can I have a pedicure at your Beauty & Relaxation studio?

Please see a podiatrist first. We can let you know whether a pedicure is suitable for you afterwards.

Can I claim on private health?

We don't offer on-the-spot claiming. If your extras cover includes podiatry, you can claim with your fund using your itemised receipt. Check with your fund about your level of cover.

Do I need a referral?

No. You can book directly with a podiatrist.
