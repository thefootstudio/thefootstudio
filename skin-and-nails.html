Treatment Options

 

  • Lunula cold nail laser

    A cold, low-level laser used alongside nail care and topical treatment in the management of fungal nail infection. It treats all five toenails on a foot at once and doesn’t heat or burn the skin. Read more about fungal nail treatment →

  • Mechanical debridement

    This is done by a Podiatrist during a General nail and skin Podiatry treatment. It reduces thickened nails and helps topical treatments penetrate. Read more about fungal nail treatment →

  • Onyfix Correction System

    A thin composite brace that gently guides an involuted or ingrown nail into a flatter shape as it grows. Read more about ingrown toenail treatment →

  • Keryflex

    A composite resin that restores the appearance of an individual’s natural nails. Remodelling an individual’s damaged nails affected by defects or trauma.

  • Minor nail and skin procedures

    Partial nail avulsion: a partial removal of a section of nail and its connecting matrix (root) under local anaesthetic. Read more about ingrown toenail surgery →

    Total nail avulsion: complete removal of the nail and matrix.

    Falknor’s needling: for stubborn plantar warts, done under local anaesthetic to help your immune system recognise and respond to the virus. Read more about plantar warts →

  • Topical treatments

    Including treatments for fungal nails and skin, and plantar warts. Fungal nails →   Plantar warts →

  • Further referral to GP/specialist

    Blood tests, oral medications.

  • Education and advice

    Understanding what is going on is a big part of your care. Sometimes the best option is no treatment at all: a clear explanation, practical advice and a plan you can manage yourself. Explore our Education page →

Frequently asked questions.

Do I need a referral?

A referral is not usually required for a private podiatry appointment.

A referral may be needed when accessing podiatry through Medicare or another funded program. Please check the requirements of your individual arrangement before booking.

Does an ingrown toenail procedure hurt?

The toe is numbed with a local anaesthetic before the procedure, so most people feel the injection and then pressure rather than pain. Your podiatrist will talk you through aftercare and pain relief.

How long does it take to treat fungal nails?

Toenails grow slowly, so improvement is gradual. A big toenail can take 12 to 18 months to grow out fully, so treatment is usually a plan over several months with regular reviews.

Are your instruments sterilised?

Yes. Our instruments are cleaned and sterilised in an autoclave between every patient, in line with Australian infection control standards.

Should I remove nail polish before my appointment?

Please remove nail polish before appointments where we need to assess or treat your toenails, so we can see the nails clearly.

Will private health insurance cover my appointment?

Podiatry may be included under extras or ancillary cover, but rebates vary between insurers and policies. Please check directly with your health fund before your appointment.
