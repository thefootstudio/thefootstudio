Team Fastest Lap: What Running Around Australia Asks of Your Feet

Around 14,000 kilometres. Around 90 kilometres a day. 155 days. In April 2027, Dr Ethan Stirrat will set off from Optus Stadium in Perth to run a lap of Australia, and we’re so proud to be part of the team helping him get around it.

Meet Ethan and Hurt to Help

Ethan is a doctor at Sir Charles Gairdner Hospital, an ultra marathon runner, and the founder of Hurt to Help. He grew up in Western Australia’s Eastern Wheatbelt, and started the charity in Narembeen in 2023.

Hurt to Help connects athletes taking on endurance challenges with Australians going through the toughest moments of their lives, from medical crises and accidents to domestic violence and natural disasters. Athletes push their physical limits, and the community’s donations go directly to the families who need them.

The Great Aussie Lap

The Great Aussie Lap is Ethan’s biggest challenge yet. He’ll run around Australia on the A1 highways, starting and finishing at Optus Stadium, from Saturday 3 April 2027. He’s aiming to finish in 155 days, as an official Fastest Known Time record attempt, and to raise $1,000,000 for Hurt to Help recipients.

Team Fastest Lap

Behind every big endurance challenge is a team. Team Fastest Lap is the group of health professionals supporting Ethan, including his coach, physiotherapist, dietitian, exercise physiologist, crew and podiatry team, all working together towards one big goal.

“When Ethan shared this big, crazy goal, I was so excited to get behind it. Being the podiatry coordinator and working alongside his sports team has been incredibly rewarding, and I’ve learnt so much.”

Laura Kelly, Director and Podiatrist, The Foot Studio

What sports podiatry really looks like

When people hear “sports podiatry”, they often picture running shoes and orthotics. Those are part of it, but supporting an athlete through something like this means looking after the whole athlete.

We’ve divided Ethan’s podiatry care into three areas, each led by podiatrists working in their specific podiatry genius zone:

  • Skin and nails: blisters, wounds, toenails, skin infections and keeping skin healthy under huge daily load.
  • Pain and movement: how his feet, legs and body handle the load, day after day.
  • Orthotics and footwear: the right shoes, socks and supports for the conditions and the kilometres.

Laura coordinates the podiatry team, and everyone comes together with Ethan’s wider sports team at regular meetings.

“It’s been really nice to show colleagues and the wider community what sports podiatry really means. It’s not all glamorous footwear, orthotic design features and musculoskeletal care. It really is treating the whole athlete.”

Laura Kelly, Director and Podiatrist, The Foot Studio

What big events ask of your feet

Feet do an enormous amount of work in ultra endurance events. In one study of runners in multi-day ultramarathons, skin problems made up around three quarters of all the injuries and illnesses recorded (Krabak et al., Medicine and Science in Sports and Exercise, 2011). Here’s some of what athletes like Ethan plan for.

Blisters

Blisters form when friction and pressure repeatedly move the skin, especially when it’s warm and damp. Fit, socks, managing moisture and caring for hot spots early all matter, and the plan is different for every athlete.

Toenails

Thousands of steps, swelling feet and long descents mean toes can repeatedly knock the end of the shoe. That can lead to bruising under the nail, and nails that lift or come off. Nail care and the right fit are part of the plan.

Skin infections and wounds

Warm, sweaty feet are the kind of environment fungal infections like, and skin that stays wet for long periods becomes soft and more fragile. Small breaks in the skin can become infected, so they need to be cleaned, covered and watched closely.

Swelling and changing feet

Feet often swell over long days on the move, so the shoe that fits on day one may not fit the same way on day 40. Having a few options matters, which is a big part of why we love a shoe rotation.

Load, day after day

Running around 90 kilometres a day asks a lot of the feet and legs. Managing that load alongside the coach, physio and the rest of the team is where pain and movement care, footwear and orthotics come together.

Lessons for everyday movers

You don’t need to run around Australia to take something from this. Whether it’s your first fun run, a weekend hike or a long day on your feet at work, the same principles apply: look after your skin and nails, get your footwear right for what you’re doing, and look at the whole person, not just the foot.
